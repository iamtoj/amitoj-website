## The Third Enlightenment

Writing and print let us externalize memory — put thoughts outside our heads, preserve them, share them across distance and time. Digital systems let us externalize computation — calculations we'd never finish by hand happen in milliseconds. Now artificial intelligence lets us externalize cognition itself: pattern recognition, judgment under uncertainty, the capacity to hold complexity we couldn't track alone.

Each transition changed what we could do. But more than that, each changed who we could become. The question we face now is whether we'll navigate this one wisely — whether we'll use these new capacities to see more clearly or just process more efficiently. Whether we'll extend our judgment or replace it.

That's what I write about. That's what the research examines. And that's what the advising work helps organizations figure out before they commit to architectures they can't reverse.

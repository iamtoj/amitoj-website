# Breath: The New Science of a Lost Art

**Author:** James Nestor
**Published:** 2020

## WHY I RECOMMEND THIS

I teach yoga at a business school. That sentence alone signals a gap most institutions ignore: the body as data source, the breath as interface between voluntary and involuntary, the physical as legitimate path to insight.

Nestor's book gave me language for what I already knew from practice. When I guide executives through pranayama, I watch blood pressure drop in real time. When I cue nasal breathing during a difficult pose, I see the nervous system shift. This is not mysticism. It is measurable.

What struck me most: the 5.5-second breath pattern that researchers identified as optimal matches the rhythm of rosary prayers, Buddhist mantras, Hindu chanting, and indigenous ceremonies across cultures. Traditions developed independently, millennia apart, converged on the same physiology. Spiritual practice and empirical optimization were never separate.

---

## THE BOOK

Modern humans breathe wrong. Forty percent suffer chronic nasal obstruction; half are habitual mouth breathers. This dysfunction underlies an epidemic of modern ailments, from anxiety to sleep apnea, ADHD to asthma.

Nestor's central claim: no matter what we eat, how much we exercise, how resilient our genes, none of it will matter unless we breathe correctly. The solution is not complex. Close your mouth. Breathe through your nose. Breathe slowly. Breathe less. Ancient traditions knew this. Modern science is catching up.

## PASSAGES THAT STAYED WITH ME

> "The greatest indicator of lifespan wasn't genetics, diet, or daily activity level, but lung capacity."

The Framingham Study followed 5,200 subjects for over two decades. Lung capacity as proxy for overall health, not as afterthought.

> "Carbon dioxide is, in fact, a more fundamental component of living matter than is oxygen."

This reverses everything we assume. CO2 is not waste; it is the signal that tells cells to release oxygen.

> "Japanese, African, Hawaiian, Native American, Buddhist, Taoist, and Christian cultures all somehow developed the same prayer techniques requiring the same breathing patterns."

Independent convergence. Spiritual practice encoding physiological optima without knowing the mechanism.

> "Inhaling air through the mouth decreases pressure, which causes the soft tissues in the back of the mouth to become loose and flex inward. Mouthbreathing begets more mouthbreathing."

The vicious cycle explained. Structure follows function.

<h2 class="text-sm text-ink-muted uppercase tracking-wider mb-4">Why I Recommend This</h2>
<div class="prose prose-lg">
<p>Springett’s model gave me language for a real variation: the same contemplative instruction can land differently for different people. The map does not explain any particular breakthrough or breakdown on its own, and I would not use it as a diagnosis. Its narrower value is asking whether a practice fits a person’s present conditions.</p>
</div>
</section>

<!-- THE BOOK -->
<section class="mb-12">
<h2 class="text-sm text-ink-muted uppercase tracking-wider mb-4">The Book</h2>
<div class="prose prose-lg">
<p>Springett presents a nine-stage model of consciousness, from what she calls dreamlike unawareness to enlightenment. She uses therapeutic cases and Buddhist developmental maps to argue that a contemplative practice should be considered in relation to a person’s conditions rather than applied uniformly.</p>
<p>I read the stages as a provisional vocabulary, not a validated assessment or a hierarchy I can use to place another person. The cases show how Springett interprets differences; they do not establish that the model diagnoses those differences.</p>
</div>
</section>

<!-- PASSAGES -->
<section class="mb-12">
<h2 class="text-sm text-ink-muted uppercase tracking-wider mb-4">Passages That Stayed With Me</h2>
<div class="prose prose-lg">
<p>The claim I would examine is narrower than the full stairway: the same instruction may not fit every person or every set of conditions.</p>
<p>The model can prompt that question. It cannot answer it clinically or determine a person’s place on a universal scale.</p>
</div>
</section>

<h2 class="text-sm text-ink-muted uppercase tracking-wider mb-4">What Stayed</h2>
<div class="prose prose-lg">
<p>Every model I had built assumed that its job was to represent a world already sitting there. Rorty makes that assumption, not the accuracy of any particular model, the object of attack.</p>
<p>Once representation stops being the foundation, vocabularies can be judged by the work they enable. That does not make every vocabulary equally useful. It changes what usefulness has to establish.</p>
</div>
</section>

<section class="mb-12">
<h2 class="text-sm text-ink-muted uppercase tracking-wider mb-4">The Argument</h2>
<div class="prose prose-lg">
<p>Rorty reconstructs the picture of mind as a mirror containing representations that can be more or less accurate. He argues that this picture turned philosophy into a search for foundations.</p>
<p>Working through Quine and Sellars, he relocates knowledge and justification to social-linguistic practice. His alternative is edification: replacing exhausted vocabularies and keeping inquiry open rather than finding a final representation of reality.</p>
</div>
</section>

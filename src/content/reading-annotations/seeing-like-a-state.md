<h2 class="text-sm text-ink-muted uppercase tracking-wider mb-4">What Stayed</h2>
<div class="prose prose-lg">
<p>An org chart can record reporting lines without recording who knows what, who trusts whom, or which exceptions keep work moving. Scott calls this omitted practical knowledge <em>mētis</em>.</p>
<p>His question is not whether formal representation is avoidable. States need legibility to act. The question is what a legibility project destroys when it mistakes the part it can record for the whole system.</p>
</div>
</section>

<section class="mb-12">
<h2 class="text-sm text-ink-muted uppercase tracking-wider mb-4">The Argument</h2>
<div class="prose prose-lg">
<p>Scott follows efforts to make societies visible and administrable through surnames, property maps, scientific forestry, and planned cities. These schemes can create the order they claim merely to describe.</p>
<p>Catastrophe, in his account, requires a conjunction: administrative simplification, high-modernist confidence, coercive power, and a civil society unable to resist. The failure is structural rather than the result of simplification alone.</p>
</div>

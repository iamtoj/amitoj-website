<h2 class="text-sm text-ink-muted uppercase tracking-wider mb-4">What Stayed</h2>
<div class="prose prose-lg">
<p>Said and Barenboim describe the West-Eastern Divan Orchestra as a project against ignorance, not a project for peace. The distinction matters. Peace promises an outcome; opposition to ignorance names work the participants can actually perform.</p>
<p>Their conversations show understanding without agreement through a shared practice. The orchestra offers a setting in which another person’s position can become intelligible without being absorbed into one’s own.</p>
</div>
</section>

<section class="mb-12">
<h2 class="text-sm text-ink-muted uppercase tracking-wider mb-4">The Argument</h2>
<div class="prose prose-lg">
<p>The book collects conversations between Edward Said and Daniel Barenboim about music, interpretation, Wagner, and the relation between art and politics. Their friendship and the orchestra provide the recurring case.</p>
<p>Musical performance requires difference, attention, and coordination without demanding that every part become the same. They use that experience to ask what dialogue can accomplish when agreement remains unavailable.</p>
</div>

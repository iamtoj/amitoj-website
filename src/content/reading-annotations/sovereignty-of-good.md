<h2 class="text-sm text-ink-muted uppercase tracking-wider mb-4">What Stayed</h2>
<div class="prose prose-lg">
<p>Choice-centered ethics starts too late. By the time a decision appears, attention has already determined which features of the situation can enter the choice.</p>
<p>Murdoch’s line is exact: “I can only choose within the world I can see.” Moral work therefore includes the patient correction of vision before any public act announces itself as moral.</p>
</div>
</section>

<section class="mb-12">
<h2 class="text-sm text-ink-muted uppercase tracking-wider mb-4">The Argument</h2>
<div class="prose prose-lg">
<p>Murdoch argues against theories that locate morality primarily in discrete choices. Fantasy, ego, and inattention shape perception continuously, while freedom is exercised in small revisions of how another person or situation is seen.</p>
<p>She calls the reduction of ego’s distortion “unselfing.” The Good functions like Plato’s sun: not one more object of vision, but the light by which other things become visible.</p>
</div>

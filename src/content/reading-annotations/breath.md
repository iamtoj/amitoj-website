<section class="mb-12">
<h2 class="text-sm text-ink-muted uppercase tracking-wider mb-4">Why I Recommend This</h2>
<div class="prose prose-lg">
<p>In my own practice, changing the breath changes how effort feels. That observation does not establish a medical mechanism or a universal prescription. Nestor assembles historical practices and physiological studies to argue for slower nasal breathing; I read the book as a prompt for inquiry, not clinical guidance.</p>
</div>
</section>

<!-- THE BOOK -->
<section class="mb-12">
<h2 class="text-sm text-ink-muted uppercase tracking-wider mb-4">The Book</h2>
<div class="prose prose-lg">
<p>Nestor argues that chronic mouth breathing and nasal obstruction are common and may be associated with several health problems. Those claims vary in evidential strength, and the book is not a clinical protocol. Readers seeking treatment should discuss breathing interventions with a qualified clinician.</p>
</div>
</section>

<!-- PASSAGES -->
<section class="mb-12">
<h2 class="text-sm text-ink-muted uppercase tracking-wider mb-4">Passages That Stayed With Me</h2>
<div class="prose prose-lg">
<p>Nestor treats breathing route, pace, lung capacity, and carbon-dioxide tolerance as connected parts of physiology.</p>
<p>I would examine each connection against primary clinical evidence rather than treat an excerpt as an instruction to change treatment or practice.</p>
</div>
</section>

<h2 class="text-sm text-ink-muted uppercase tracking-wider mb-4">What Stayed</h2>
<div class="prose prose-lg">
<p>Changing my breath changes how effort feels in my own practice. Nestor gave me a set of variables for examining that experience—route, pace, lung capacity, and carbon-dioxide tolerance—without turning the experience into a treatment claim.</p>
<p>The useful question begins where the book’s synthesis ends: which proposed connections survive comparison with primary clinical evidence, for whom, and under what conditions?</p>
</div>

<section class="mb-12">
<h2 class="text-sm text-ink-muted uppercase tracking-wider mb-4">The Argument</h2>
<div class="prose prose-lg">
<p>Nestor connects historical breathing practices with physiological research and argues for slower nasal breathing. The strength of the connections varies. This is a popular investigation, not an individualized treatment plan; clinical use belongs with primary evidence and a qualified clinician.</p>
</div>

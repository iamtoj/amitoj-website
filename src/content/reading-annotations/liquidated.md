<h2 class="text-sm text-ink-muted uppercase tracking-wider mb-4">What Stayed</h2>
<div class="prose prose-lg">
<p>The usual account begins with “the market” and asks how bankers respond to it. Ho begins one step earlier: which workplace practices produce the actors who later describe their decisions as market necessity?</p>
<p>Her answer connects smartness, liquidity, job insecurity, compensation, and deal-making. The organization does not merely execute a market logic imported from outside. It trains a way of seeing, then carries that way of seeing into the companies its bankers restructure.</p>
</div>
</section>

<section class="mb-12">
<h2 class="text-sm text-ink-muted uppercase tracking-wider mb-4">The Argument</h2>
<div class="prose prose-lg">
<p>Ho’s ethnography follows investment bankers at work. She shows how a culture that celebrates liquidity and normalizes precarity shapes both employment inside Wall Street firms and advice delivered to corporate clients.</p>
<p>Compensation rewards deals in the present while many consequences arrive later. In Ho’s account, the distance between those clocks helps turn short-term extraction into an ordinary professional practice.</p>
</div>

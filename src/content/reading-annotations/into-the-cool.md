<section class="mb-12">
<h2 class="text-sm text-ink-muted uppercase tracking-wider mb-4">What Stayed</h2>
<div class="prose prose-lg">
<p>Life looks like an exception to entropy only if local order and total dissipation are counted at the same scale. Schneider and Sagan separate them. Living systems maintain local organization by moving energy and matter through themselves while exporting entropy elsewhere.</p>
<p>That is the part of the book I can carry directly. Any move from biological thermodynamics to organizational behavior is a comparison of patterns rather than the mechanism the book demonstrates.</p>
</div>
</section>

<section class="mb-12">
<h2 class="text-sm text-ink-muted uppercase tracking-wider mb-4">The Argument</h2>
<div class="prose prose-lg">
<p>Schneider and Sagan begin from gradients—differences in temperature, pressure, or chemical concentration. The second law describes their tendency to dissipate. The authors treat living systems as dissipative structures that maintain local order while accelerating dissipation.</p>
<p>Their proposed division is that thermodynamics supplies a direction under given constraints while genetics supplies a mechanism. The book uses that relation to explain how biological complexity can increase without violating the second law.</p>
</div>
</section>

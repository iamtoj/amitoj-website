<h2 class="text-sm text-ink-muted uppercase tracking-wider mb-4">What Stayed</h2>
<div class="prose prose-lg">
<p>Theise is a pathologist and a long-term Zen practitioner. The conjunction matters less than the separation he maintains. Complexity theory can describe self-organization across cells, colonies, and ecosystems. The claim that consciousness is fundamental arrives later and owes an argument of its own.</p>
<p>I return to the book at that joint, where a scientific account produces a metaphysical question without answering it.</p>
</div>
</section>

<section class="mb-12">
<h2 class="text-sm text-ink-muted uppercase tracking-wider mb-4">The Argument</h2>
<div class="prose prose-lg">
<p>Local interaction can produce coherent higher-level patterns without central control. Theise develops that account through emergence and feedback, then carries it into a picture of a living, conscious universe. The first move explains how wholes arise. It does not settle what kind of thing the whole is.</p>
</div>

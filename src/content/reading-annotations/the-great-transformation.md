<h2 class="text-sm text-ink-muted uppercase tracking-wider mb-4">What Stayed</h2>
<div class="prose prose-lg">
<p>“Self-regulating market” makes the market sound like the foundation from which politics departs. Polanyi reverses the sequence. The market had to be produced and enforced through political action before its dislocations could appear as the cost of interference with a natural order.</p>
<p>That reversal gives me a sharper question for platform work: what bodily and social conditions disappear when labor enters the model as an input? The comparison restores a historical problem without claiming that a current platform reproduces Polanyi’s cases.</p>
</div>
</section>

<section class="mb-12">
<h2 class="text-sm text-ink-muted uppercase tracking-wider mb-4">The Argument</h2>
<div class="prose prose-lg">
<p>Polanyi treats labor, land, and money as fictitious commodities because they were not produced for sale. Subjecting them fully to price would damage the social and natural conditions on which the market depends.</p>
<p>His “double movement” names market expansion and the protective responses it provokes. It is a historical account of nineteenth-century transformation rather than a rule that predicts which protective coalition will emerge.</p>
</div>

<h2 class="text-sm text-ink-muted uppercase tracking-wider mb-4">What Stayed</h2>
<div class="prose prose-lg">
<p>Calling repetition a failure to learn assumes that the past remains available to the learner. Macondo makes that assumption unstable. Names repeat, wars recur, and the banana massacre disappears from collective memory. Forgetting is not outside the cycle. It helps produce it.</p>
<p>That is why I resist using the novel as a decorative analogy for organizations. Its harder question is prior: what forms of memory would have to survive for a group to recognize that it has returned to the same place?</p>
</div>

<section class="mb-12">
<h2 class="text-sm text-ink-muted uppercase tracking-wider mb-4">The Argument</h2>
<div class="prose prose-lg">
<p>The novel follows seven generations of the Buendía family as Macondo moves from founding through expansion, exploitation, and destruction. Time circles through repeated names, dispositions, conflicts, and losses.</p>
<p>Solitude takes different forms across the family—power, beauty, obsession, and failed intimacy. The characters do not simply repeat events. They inherit conditions that make the repetitions difficult to see.</p>
</div>

<h2 class="text-sm text-ink-muted uppercase tracking-wider mb-4">Why I Recommend This</h2>
<div class="prose prose-lg">
<p>Kronman inhabits a gap between traditional theism and atheism: institutional faith can feel intellectually untenable while a disenchanted account of the world can feel spiritually incomplete. I recommend the book because he treats that tension as a philosophical problem rather than evidence that either side is simply missing something.</p>
<p>His move is simple and radical: identify God with the world itself—Deus sive Natura. On Kronman’s account, contemplative practice can then carry theological weight without requiring supernatural belief. He builds that position through a lineage including Aristotle, Spinoza, Whitman, and Nietzsche.</p>
</div>
</section>

<section class="mb-12">
<h2 class="text-sm text-ink-muted uppercase tracking-wider mb-4">The Book</h2>
<div class="prose prose-lg">
<p>At 1,100 pages spanning 2,500 years of Western thought, this is by no means a beach read. Kronman traces philosophy from Plato through Augustine and Aquinas to Kant, then reconstructs a third way between traditional theism and atheism. The core move: identifying God with "the inexhaustible and everlasting presence of the world itself."</p>
<p>Spinoza serves as the intellectual foundation with his Deus sive Natura—God or Nature, one reality. Whitman demonstrates what this looks like lived: celebrating the divinity of ordinary existence, the human body, the passing moment as containing eternity. Kronman calls this synthesis "born-again paganism"—ancient insight combined with Christian depth, compatible with modern reason and democratic values.</p>
</div>
</section>

<p class="text-sm text-ink-muted">I recognized myself immediately in that anxious wish.</p>
</blockquote>
<blockquote class="border-l-2 border-sage-400 pl-6">
<p class="text-lg italic mb-2">"A God whose inexhaustible and everlasting presence is that of the world itself."</p>
<p class="text-sm text-ink-muted">The whole book pivots on this sentence.</p>
</blockquote>
<blockquote class="border-l-2 border-sage-400 pl-6">
<p class="text-lg italic mb-2">"The loveless world of rights is the poisonous fruit of the Christian religion."</p>
<p class="text-sm text-ink-muted">Harsh but earned—Kronman traces how transcendent theology produced secular disenchantment.</p>
</blockquote>
<blockquote class="border-l-2 border-sage-400 pl-6">
<p class="text-lg italic mb-2">"Born-again paganism reconciles the longing to be close to God with the ideals of our secular age."</p>
<p class="text-sm text-ink-muted">The promise that kept me reading through 1,100 pages.</p>
</blockquote>
<blockquote class="border-l-2 border-sage-400 pl-6">
<p class="text-lg italic mb-2">"In many religions, the soul ascends to an eternal afterlife. In Kronman's pagan view, it's the universe that's eternal; when we die, we return to it. Life is a brief opportunity to look around and see where we are going."</p>
<p class="text-sm text-ink-muted">Mortality that intensifies rather than negates meaning.</p>
</blockquote>

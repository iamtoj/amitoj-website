<h2 class="text-sm text-ink-muted uppercase tracking-wider mb-4">What Stayed</h2>
<div class="prose prose-lg">
<p>Western intellectual history is often arranged as a choice: Plato or Aristotle, transcendence or immanence, reason or participation. Tarnas treats the separation itself as a stage in a longer movement rather than a mistake one side can correct.</p>
<p>That creates a demanding possibility for integration. An attempted synthesis can preserve the split it means to overcome if its categories remain unchanged. “Participatory knowing” is the name Tarnas gives his alternative; whether one civilizational dialectic explains every failure of integration remains open.</p>
</div>
</section>

<section class="mb-12">
<h2 class="text-sm text-ink-muted uppercase tracking-wider mb-4">The Argument</h2>
<div class="prose prose-lg">
<p>Tarnas traces Western thought from ancient participation through modern separation and toward a possible conscious participation. Greek philosophy, Christianity, modern science, and postmodern critique become moments in that arc.</p>
<p>His epilogue turns the historical reconstruction into an archetypal claim: critical autonomy was necessary, but it need not be the final relation between mind and world. Participation would have to retain differentiation rather than regress to undivided unity.</p>
</div>

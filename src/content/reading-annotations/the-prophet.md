<h2 class="text-sm text-ink-muted uppercase tracking-wider mb-4">What Stayed</h2>
<div class="prose prose-lg">
<p>I used to read Gibran’s images as elegant but remote: pillars standing apart to hold a temple, children as arrows released from a bow, joy and sorrow filling the same well. They name tensions I recognize more clearly now.</p>
<p>Intimacy without fusion became an actual problem to solve. Work carrying meaning without demanding guaranteed outcomes became the question. Poetic form can hold those tensions without pretending to resolve them; that is the precision I return to.</p>
</div>
</section>

<section class="mb-12">
<h2 class="text-sm text-ink-muted uppercase tracking-wider mb-4">The Argument</h2>
<div class="prose prose-lg">
<p>Almustafa prepares to leave Orphalese after twelve years and answers questions about love, marriage, children, work, freedom, pain, and death.</p>
<p>The prose poems repeatedly hold apparent opposites in one image. Love crowns and crucifies. Joy and sorrow occupy the same well. Reason and passion become rudder and sail. The form preserves the tension rather than converting it into a procedure.</p>
</div>

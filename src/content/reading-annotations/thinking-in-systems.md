<section class="mb-12">
<h2 class="text-sm text-ink-muted uppercase tracking-wider mb-4">What Stayed</h2>
<div class="prose prose-lg">
<p>When a metric changes after an intervention, the sequence is often treated as the explanation. Meadows asks what feedback the intervention altered before assigning cause.</p>
<p>Reinforcing loops can amplify a result, balancing loops can resist it, and delays can separate an action from its visible consequences. These are questions to investigate in a particular system rather than mechanisms to assign by resemblance alone.</p>
</div>
</section>

<section class="mb-12">
<h2 class="text-sm text-ink-muted uppercase tracking-wider mb-4">The Argument</h2>
<div class="prose prose-lg">
<p>Meadows builds from stocks, flows, feedback loops, and delays to recurring system traps and leverage points. Events can trigger behavior, but structure helps determine how the system responds.</p>
<p>Her hierarchy of leverage points moves from parameters toward information, rules, goals, and paradigms. The final discipline is not mastery through control. It is remaining able to revise the model while acting inside the system.</p>
</div>

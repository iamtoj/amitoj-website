<h2 class="text-sm text-ink-muted uppercase tracking-wider mb-4">What Stayed</h2>
<div class="prose prose-lg">
<p>“Unprecedented” is not a neutral description. It can remove the analogies through which an older political or regulatory question would become visible.</p>
<p>Daub restores the histories behind disruption, genius, failure, platforms, and sharing. An analogy to publishers, taxis, labor, or common carriers does not settle a present classification. It prevents novelty from deciding the question before the argument begins.</p>
</div>
</section>

<section class="mb-12">
<h2 class="text-sm text-ink-muted uppercase tracking-wider mb-4">The Argument</h2>
<div class="prose prose-lg">
<p>Daub traces seven recurring terms in Silicon Valley discourse back to older intellectual histories. His target is not novelty itself but the use of novelty to detach a present claim from prior arguments and obligations.</p>
<p>Calling a company a platform or an activity ride-sharing changes which comparisons enter the discussion. The book asks what disappears when that vocabulary is accepted on its own terms.</p>
</div>

<section class="mb-12">
<h2 class="text-sm text-ink-muted uppercase tracking-wider mb-4">What Stayed</h2>
<div class="prose prose-lg">
<p>A dashboard can remain internally green while unmeasured dependencies and relationships fail. That exposes a limit in the representation. It does not tell us which hemisphere built the dashboard or detected the failure.</p>
<p>McGilchrist gives me a vocabulary for the representational problem: narrow attention isolates and manipulates; broad attention sustains context, relation, and change. His move from that distinction to consciousness as fundamental is a further argument rather than a result supplied by the dashboard or the neuroscience alone.</p>
</div>
</section>

<section class="mb-12">
<h2 class="text-sm text-ink-muted uppercase tracking-wider mb-4">The Argument</h2>
<div class="prose prose-lg">
<p>McGilchrist distinguishes two modes of attention associated with the hemispheres. The left favors explicit parts, abstraction, and control; the right sustains living wholes, implicit meaning, and context. Both are necessary, but he argues that their relation has become inverted.</p>
<p>He then carries the distinction from neuroscience into questions of time, matter, consciousness, value, and the sacred. The scale of that extension is the book’s project and also the place where its claims require separate evaluation.</p>
</div>

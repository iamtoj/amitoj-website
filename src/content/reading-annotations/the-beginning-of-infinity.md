<h2 class="text-sm text-ink-muted uppercase tracking-wider mb-4">What Stayed</h2>
<div class="prose prose-lg">
<p>An explanation that can absorb every possible outcome has not explained the outcome. Deutsch’s test is whether the account is hard to vary while preserving what it claims to explain.</p>
<p>The test audits the foundation before prediction begins. If opposite evidence can be accommodated by changing a loose story around the result, the story has not yet constrained the world.</p>
</div>
</section>

<section class="mb-12">
<h2 class="text-sm text-ink-muted uppercase tracking-wider mb-4">The Argument</h2>
<div class="prose prose-lg">
<p>Deutsch places explanatory knowledge at the center of progress. Good explanations constrain their own variation; criticism and testing can therefore expose where they fail.</p>
<p>He extends that epistemology into a broad optimism: problems persist because knowledge is incomplete, not because improvement has reached a final boundary. The hard-to-vary criterion can be used independently of accepting every extension of that optimism.</p>
</div>

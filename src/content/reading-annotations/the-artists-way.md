<section class="mb-12">
<h2 class="text-sm text-ink-muted uppercase tracking-wider mb-4">Why I Recommend This</h2>
<div class="prose prose-lg">
<p>Cameron asks for three handwritten pages each morning. In my first month, recurring concerns became easier to see on the page. That is a personal observation, not evidence that the practice works generally.</p>
<p>Her core reframe is creativity as recovery—removing obstacles to what is already there. I return to the practice as a way to inspect what appears, not a guaranteed route to creative recovery.</p>
</div>
</section>

<!-- THE BOOK -->
<section class="mb-12">
<h2 class="text-sm text-ink-muted uppercase tracking-wider mb-4">The Book</h2>
<div class="prose prose-lg">
<p>Cameron argues that creativity is a fundamental human capacity we've been systematically blocked from accessing. The refusal to be creative is self-will, counter to our true nature. Cameron treats creative blocks as spiritual wounds stemming from fear, criticism, and cultural conditioning. Recovery requires spiritual practice, enacted through concrete daily rituals that reconnect us to creative flow.</p>
<p>The book presents a twelve-week program built on two foundational practices: Morning Pages (three pages of handwritten stream-of-consciousness writing each morning) and Artist Dates (weekly solo expeditions for creative nourishment). These practices are the creative life itself. Process matters more than product. The goal is living creatively, using the gifts we've been given.</p>
</div>
</section>

<h2 class="text-sm text-ink-muted uppercase tracking-wider mb-4">What Stayed</h2>
<div class="prose prose-lg">
<p>When two people disagree about data, we usually assume they are interpreting the same object differently. Kuhn questions the “same object.” A paradigm helps determine what counts as a problem, an anomaly, or relevant evidence before interpretation begins.</p>
<p>The foundation is therefore not neutral observation followed by theory. Observation is already organized by a framework. The useful question is not merely which explanation fits the facts, but which facts each explanation makes available for comparison.</p>
</div>
</section>

<section class="mb-12">
<h2 class="text-sm text-ink-muted uppercase tracking-wider mb-4">The Argument</h2>
<div class="prose prose-lg">
<p>Normal science solves puzzles inside an accepted paradigm. Anomalies accumulate, confidence weakens, and a competing framework can reorganize the field’s problems, methods, and standards.</p>
<p>Kuhn calls the resulting transition a scientific revolution. Paradigms are not freely interchangeable descriptions of one fixed world; they help constitute the scientific world their practitioners can investigate. Comparison remains possible, but it cannot begin from a framework-free vantage point.</p>
</div>

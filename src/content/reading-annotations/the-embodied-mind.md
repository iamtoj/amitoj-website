<h2 class="text-sm text-ink-muted uppercase tracking-wider mb-4">What Stayed</h2>
<div class="prose prose-lg">
<p>Sitting in meditation long enough, I stopped finding the watcher I assumed was there. Breath, sensation, and mental activity appeared and vanished without a stable observer directing them.</p>
<p>Varela, Thompson, and Rosch place that report beside an enactive account of cognition. Organism and environment bring forth a world through their history of interaction. The comparison makes two inquiries mutually legible without letting either serve as proof of the other.</p>
</div>
</section>

<section class="mb-12">
<h2 class="text-sm text-ink-muted uppercase tracking-wider mb-4">The Argument</h2>
<div class="prose prose-lg">
<p>Cognitivism and connectionism differ in mechanism, but the authors argue that both can retain the same foundation: a pre-given mind representing a pre-given world.</p>
<p>Enaction rejects that starting point. Mind and world arise through embodied action and structural coupling. Buddhist accounts of no substantial self then become a disciplined comparison rather than a spiritual decoration on cognitive science.</p>
</div>
</section>

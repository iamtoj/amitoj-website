<h2 class="text-sm text-ink-muted uppercase tracking-wider mb-4">What Stayed</h2>
<div class="prose prose-lg">
<p>If the mind begins by treating a conceptual self as the thing to be protected, then uses that same mind to investigate the self, the inquiry becomes circular. Ralston’s method begins by withdrawing the assumed answer.</p>
<p>“Not knowing” is not ignorance as a conclusion. It is a posture that keeps the investigation from returning immediately to the concepts it was meant to examine.</p>
</div>
</section>

<section class="mb-12">
<h2 class="text-sm text-ink-muted uppercase tracking-wider mb-4">The Argument</h2>
<div class="prose prose-lg">
<p>Ralston connects full-contact martial arts with an inquiry into consciousness and direct experience. He argues that whatever is treated as the self will be defended and maintained.</p>
<p>When self and mind are identified, the mind can become the protector of its own account. His exercises ask the reader to notice that loop directly. His larger claim that self-survival causes all suffering remains a claim inside his framework rather than a premise the method requires.</p>
</div>
</section>

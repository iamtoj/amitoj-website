<h2 class="text-sm text-ink-muted uppercase tracking-wider mb-4">What Stayed</h2>
<div class="prose prose-lg">
<p>The returning bombers were not a representative sample. The missing bullet holes were on the planes that did not return. Wald’s correction is more than a memorable example: before interpreting visible data, ask what process determined which observations became visible.</p>
<p>Ellenberg’s larger point is that mathematics extends common sense by disciplining it. Calculation matters, but judgment begins one step earlier—with deciding whether the calculation answers the right question.</p>
</div>
</section>

<section class="mb-12">
<h2 class="text-sm text-ink-muted uppercase tracking-wider mb-4">The Argument</h2>
<div class="prose prose-lg">
<p>Ellenberg develops mathematical thinking through linearity, inference, expectation, regression, and existence. His cases include bomber armor, statistical significance, lotteries, and the impossibility of a perfect voting system.</p>
<p>The book treats mathematics as a civic capacity rather than a specialist performance: a way to expose hidden structure, test an intuition, and recognize what a formal result cannot decide by itself.</p>
</div>

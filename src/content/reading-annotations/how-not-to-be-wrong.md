<h2 class="text-sm text-ink-muted uppercase tracking-wider mb-4">Why I Recommend This</h2>
<div class="prose prose-lg">
<p>The bullet holes on returning WWII bombers clustered in the fuselage and wings. The military's instinct was to add armor there. Abraham Wald said: armor the engines—the planes hit there didn't come back.</p>
<p>Ellenberg opens with this story and it sets the tone for everything that follows. Mathematics as a science of not being wrong. Survivorship bias as the paradigm case of how intuition misleads and systematic thinking corrects. This book addresses the gap between calculation and judgment—demonstrating that mathematical thinking extends common sense, and that mathematical humility (knowing what you can't calculate) matters as much as technical skill.</p>
</div>
</section>

<!-- THE BOOK -->
<section class="mb-12">
<h2 class="text-sm text-ink-muted uppercase tracking-wider mb-4">The Book</h2>
<div class="prose prose-lg">
<p>Ellenberg argues that mathematics is "the extension of common sense by other means"—a systematic way of not being wrong refined through centuries of collective effort. The book challenges the dominant educational paradigm where mathematics appears as rote memorization, revealing mathematical thinking as an essential tool for navigating a world saturated with data, shaped by algorithms, and characterized by nonlinear relationships.</p>
<p>Through five domains (linearity, inference, expectation, regression, and existence), Ellenberg demonstrates how mathematical thinking reveals hidden structures beneath surface complexity. He covers survivorship bias using WWII bomber armor placement, the replication crisis in psychology, MIT students who beat the Massachusetts lottery through expected value calculations, and Arrow's impossibility theorem proving that perfect voting systems cannot exist. Ellenberg repositions mathematics as civic capability—systematic thinking that augments judgment, available to anyone willing to follow the reasoning.</p>
</div>
</section>

<section class="mb-12">
<h2 class="text-sm text-ink-muted uppercase tracking-wider mb-4">Passages That Stayed With Me</h2>
<div class="space-y-6">
<blockquote class="border-l-2 border-sage-400 pl-6">
<p class="text-lg italic mb-2">"Mathematics is the extension of common sense by other means."</p>
<p class="text-sm text-ink-muted">The extension runs in both directions—math formalizes intuition, and intuition checks formalism.</p>
</blockquote>
<blockquote class="border-l-2 border-sage-400 pl-6">
<p class="text-lg italic mb-2">"The armour doesn't go where the bullet holes are. It goes where the bullet holes aren't."</p>
<p class="text-sm text-ink-muted">The missing bullet holes were on the missing planes.</p>
</blockquote>
<blockquote class="border-l-2 border-sage-400 pl-6">
<p class="text-lg italic mb-2">"Working an integral or performing a linear regression is something a computer can do quite effectively. Understanding whether the result makes sense—or deciding whether the method is the right one to use in the first place—requires a guiding human hand."</p>
<p class="text-sm text-ink-muted">The computer runs the integral. You decide whether the integral was the right question.</p>
</blockquote>
<blockquote class="border-l-2 border-sage-400 pl-6">
<p class="text-lg italic mb-2">"One of the most painful parts of teaching mathematics is seeing students damaged by the cult of the genius. The genius cult tells students it's not worth doing mathematics unless you're the best at mathematics, because those special few are the only ones whose contributions matter."</p>
<p class="text-sm text-ink-muted">The genius myth narrows who gets to think quantitatively.</p>
</blockquote>
<blockquote class="border-l-2 border-sage-400 pl-6">
<p class="text-lg italic mb-2">"It's not enough that the data be consistent with your theory; they have to be inconsistent with the negation of your theory, the dreaded null hypothesis."</p>
<p class="text-sm text-ink-muted">Statistical significance is the beginning of inquiry, not the end.</p>
</blockquote>
</div>
</section>

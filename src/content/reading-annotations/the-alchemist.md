<h2 class="text-sm text-ink-muted uppercase tracking-wider mb-4">What Stayed</h2>
<div class="prose prose-lg">
<p>I read this on a flight years ago and began skeptical of its reputation. The scene I kept was Santiago learning to listen to the wind.</p>
<p>I do not take the universe’s conspiracy on behalf of desire as a causal law. The story’s more durable sequence is that a journey changes the capacity with which a person returns. The treasure can be near the beginning and still remain unavailable to the person who began there.</p>
</div>
</section>

<section class="mb-12">
<h2 class="text-sm text-ink-muted uppercase tracking-wider mb-4">The Argument</h2>
<div class="prose prose-lg">
<p>Santiago dreams of treasure at the pyramids, sells his flock, crosses the desert, falls in love, and learns to read omens. He eventually finds that the treasure was buried near the place from which he started.</p>
<p>Coelho calls the sought path a Personal Legend and places it inside a world animated by signs and synchronicity. The fable treats action as the means by which the seeker becomes capable of recognizing what the journey was for.</p>
</div>

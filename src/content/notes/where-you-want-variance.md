Early on, I tried to specify everything in a system I was building to process information. I wrote rules for classifications and handlers for edge cases, and the system followed them so faithfully that it became rigid enough to miss what I wanted.

I had treated every judgment I could articulate as a judgment I should turn into a rule. A rule can improve a system only relative to an objective, yet I had treated rule-following itself as evidence of improvement.

---

Every objective fixes something and leaves something else open. A report must be finished by Friday, while the time, place, and method remain matters of discretion.

Call the first part the _that_ and the second the _how_. The distinction recurs at every level: “use a template” can become a _that_, while the choice of which sections deserve emphasis remains a _how_. Any _how_ can be decomposed until one of its parts becomes another requirement.

The boundary moves as judgment becomes articulable. A choice I cannot state remains in the _how_; once I can state it, I can convert it into a _that_ and make the process more predictable. But articulation answers only whether specification is possible. It does not answer whether the objective should absorb the judgment.

---

Each conversion removes a place where outcomes can differ. That is variance reduction.

For a long time, I treated the reduction as an uncomplicated gain because reliability often requires it. An aircraft engine should not depend on who tightened the bolt or what mood that person was in.

Art reveals the limit. If a procedure specifies the work so completely that any trained person produces the same result, the maker contributes nothing the procedure has left open. The same reduction that protects an engine can erase the point of a painting.

The cases differ because their objectives differ. Variance and consistency have no value apart from the work: the objective decides which differences count as error and which carry the result.

---

That returned me to the system. I needed classifications dependable enough to be useful while still leaving somewhere for an edge case to remain an edge case.

The rules and handlers made the existing categories more consistent, but together they made the system rigid enough to miss what I wanted. It had become reliable at preserving a specification that defeated its purpose.

The fix was knowing where to stop. Some instructions remained rules; others became principles—enough direction to guide a choice without deciding it in advance. Some _hows_ remained _hows_ even after I could imagine decomposing them further.

The test is where failure appears. When consistency is part of the objective, variation is the error. When the objective depends on noticing that a case does not fit the categories, enforced consistency hides the error instead.

---

The ability to specify a judgment does not settle whether it belongs in the specification. In my system, the remaining _hows_ are not unfinished rules. They are the places where an edge case can still put pressure on the specification.

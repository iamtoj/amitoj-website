# Voice Diagnosis: Why amitoj.co Sounds Like AI

**Date:** 2026-02-23
**Problem:** Website essays sound like AI, not like the author
**Source evidence:** Voice calibration report (`templates/voice-calibration-report.md`), website voice analysis (`templates/website-voice-analysis.md`), personal voice profile (`templates/personalvoiceprofile.md`)

---

## The Core Problem

The website performs thinking. The author's real writing (papers, memos) *does* thinking. The difference is audible.

AI is good at producing confident, symmetrical, quotable prose. It is bad at holding genuine tension, qualifying precisely, and arriving at verdicts through sustained engagement. The website essays use the moves AI is good at. The author's actual voice uses the moves AI is bad at.

---

## What the Website Does (AI-Sounding Moves)

These are the patterns that make the essays sound generated:

### 1. Bold declarative openings
> "Markets are miraculous coordination machines."
> "There is no algorithm for determining what the algorithm should maximize."

The author's real voice almost never does this. Papers build to claims through generous engagement with prior work, then pivot when the gap appears.

### 2. Dual-audience TLDR framings
> "For people: Before asking 'how do I win?'..."
> "For organizations: Markets coordinate brilliantly..."

This is a performed move. The author never uses it in papers or memos. It claims authority over two audiences simultaneously without earning it through the essay's argument.

### 3. Staccato logic-building chains
> "Markets are miraculous coordination machines. When tin becomes scarce somewhere in the world, prices rise. Shippers adjust their routes. Manufacturers find substitutes. Consumers reduce consumption."

This is didactic, not analytical. It teaches by building a chain of simple declarative sentences. The author's real voice uses colon specifications and concessive long sentences that hold tension.

### 4. Symmetrical constructions
> "Acceptance without agency is resignation. Agency without acceptance is violence against reality."
> "Markets can coordinate activity *within* the boundaries. They cannot determine where those boundaries should be."

Perfect symmetry is an AI tell. The author's real prose holds asymmetric tension — concessive clauses, "while" constructions, paired em-dashes that complicate rather than balance.

### 5. Aphoristic closings
> "The right direction is not given by the mechanism. It is what the mechanism presupposes."
> "Strategic choice is not given. It is constructed."

These are packages. They compress insight into quotable form. The author's best closings (in personal essays) are unresolved questions. In papers, they're final-layer implications that extend rather than compress.

### 6. The constitutive loop as set piece
> "What becomes measurable becomes salient; what becomes salient becomes legitimate; what becomes legitimate becomes binding..."

Performed recursion. The chain is too clean. Real recursive arguments in the author's papers hold the feedback loop in a single concessive sentence rather than performing it as a rhetorical cascade.

### 7. "Not X but Y" constructions
> "The question is not only 'how do we win?' but 'what game should we be playing?'"

Zero tolerance. The author is actively reducing these in papers. They are the signature AI-ism: confident reframing that sounds precise but often just restates the same claim twice.

### 8. Performed introspection openings
> "I keep returning to a pattern..."
> "I've been thinking about this gap for months now."
> "The word I keep returning to is peer."

These narrate the author's cognitive habits TO the reader. They stage authenticity rather than making intellectual moves. Every Medium post opens this way. The author's real first-person moves are epistemic claims about IDEAS ("I'm not confident the two failure modes are symmetrical," "if you allow me to sketch broadly"). One advances the argument. The other decorates it.

The distinction: first-person that qualifies a claim or addresses the reader = real. First-person that narrates the author's process of thinking = performed.

## What the Author's Real Voice Does (From Calibration Report)

These are the measured patterns from published papers (Gilded Shelter, Attention Allocation, Right Direction) and I&A memos:

### Sentence-level
- **Average 17-20 words** (not 25-35). Shorter than the website. More readable.
- **Colon specification** is the primary claim delivery: "claim: specification." More frequent than em-dashes for argumentative work.
- **"That is," restater**: mid-sentence precision tool that says "let me make sure you tracked that."
- **Paired em-dash parentheticals**: clarifying clause inserted mid-sentence without breaking flow. 37-44 per paper. Different from definitional em-dashes.
- **Concessive long sentences**: longest sentences hold two ideas in tension using "while," "though," or paired em-dashes. Up to 77 words.

### Paragraph-level
- **60-110 words average** (not 150-300). Much shorter than prescribed.
- **"This X" bridges** dominate paragraph openings: "This reframing clarifies," "This emphasis matters because." 13-26 per paper.
- **Question clusters as pivots**: 2-3 genuine questions at the end of exposition paragraphs, marking the turn from summary to critique. "But which values? Against which metric?"
- **Scope clarification blocks**: "By X I do not mean..." at critical junctures. Not obsessive, but present.

### Rhetorical
- **Generous-then-gap pivot**: build the strongest version of a position, acknowledge its power, then identify the gap. Never straw-man. The author's signature move.
- **Verdict after evidence**: in memos, direct judgment after sustained engagement. "Rogers's case earns a verdict."
- **Inline questions as analytical tools**: 13-21 per paper. Genuine, not ornamental. Carry argumentative weight.
- **Self-conscious recursion**: signals awareness that the argument's structure mirrors its content.
- **First-person epistemic hedges**: "I am not confident," "strikes me as genuinely open." Routes uncertainty through the person, not the claim.
- **Contractions**: permitted. Register marker distinguishing website/personal from academic.

### What's absent from the author's real writing
- No bold declarative openings
- No dual-audience framings
- No staccato chains
- No symmetrical constructions
- No aphoristic closings
- No constitutive loops as rhetorical set pieces
- No performed introspection openings ("I keep returning to," "I've been thinking about")
- Very few "Not X but Y" (trending downward: 15 → 12 → 7 across three papers)
- No forbidden words (obviously, clearly, interestingly, surprisingly, perhaps, maybe, in fact)

---

## The Fix

Rewrite the website using the author's **paper moves** at the **website altitude**.

Paper moves = genuine questions, colon specifications, concessive tension, scope qualifications, earned verdicts, generous-then-gap pivots.

Website altitude = contractions, first-person intellectual confession, shorter paragraphs than papers, cases as illustrations, 1-3 thinkers woven mid-sentence.

The result should sound like a mind thinking on the page — not performing insight, not teaching, not packaging. Thinking.

## Blog Posts vs. Essays

The blog posts (Dot Collector, Tacit Knowledge, Agent Polities, Multiple Minds, Where You Want Variance, Third Enlightenment blog) are already much closer to the author's real voice. They start with specific scenes, hold genuine tension, have real vulnerability, and end with unresolved questions.

The three main essays (Right Direction, Strategic Time, Architecture of Commitment) are the most AI-sounding — derived from academic papers but stripped of the moves that make the papers work.

The Third Enlightenment page is also AI-sounding (the "formula" section, the "Three Modes" section, the symmetrical constructions).
